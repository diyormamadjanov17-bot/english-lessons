Lesson 1 — Overview
What is the Present Perfect?
It links a past action to the present moment — we care about the result, not the exact time.

✅ Life experience

I have visited Japan.
She has never tried sushi.

🔗 Result in the present

He has lost his keys. (he can't find them now)
It has stopped raining.

🕐 Unfinished time period

I have studied a lot today.
We haven't eaten this week.

📌 Signal words

ever, never, already, yet, just,
recently, so far, this week / year
Lesson 2 — Structure
How to Build the Sentence
Always use have / has + the past participle (3rd form) of the verb.
✅ Subject + have / has + past participle
I have seen that film.  |  She has eaten breakfast.
❌ Subject + have / has not + past participle
I haven't seen it.  |  She hasn't eaten.
❓ Have / Has + Subject + past participle?
Have you seen it?  |  Has she eaten?
I / you / we / they → have
I have finished. / Have you finished?
Short form: I've, you've, we've, they've
he / she / it → has
She has arrived. / Has he arrived?
Short form: he's, she's, it's + past participle
Lesson 3 — Past Participle
Regular & Irregular Verbs
The past participle (3rd form) is crucial — regular verbs add -ed, irregulars must be memorised.
Regular → add -ed
work → worked
visit → visited  |  finish → finished
Irregular — common ones
go → gone  |  see → seen
eat → eaten  |  write → written
More irregulars
make → made  |  take → taken
buy → bought  |  have → had
Same in all forms
put → put  |  cut → cut
read → read (pronounced "red")
  • I have written three emails today.
  • They have bought a new car.
  • She hasn't made a decision yet.
Lesson 4 — Signal Words
Key Words & Where They Go
Certain adverbs always appear with Present Perfect — learn their position in the sentence.
✅
already — before the past participle I have already finished my homework. (sooner than expected)
⚠️
yet — at the end (negatives & questions) Have you eaten yet?  |  She hasn't called yet.
⚡
just — before the past participle He has just arrived. (very recently)
🌍
ever / never — before the past participle Have you ever tried skydiving?  |  I've never been to Spain.
📅
for / since — time duration / starting point I've lived here for 5 years.  |  She's worked there since 2019.
Lesson 5 — Common Confusion
Present Perfect vs Past Simple
This is the trickiest part. The difference is whether the time is mentioned or matters.

✅ Present Perfect

  • No specific time given
  • Result matters now
  • Life experience
  • With: ever, never, already, yet, just
"I have lost my phone."
"She has read that book."
VS

🕰️ Past Simple

  • Specific time stated
  • Finished, closed action
  • Story / sequence of events
  • With: yesterday, last week, in 2010, ago
"I lost my phone yesterday."
"She read it last year."

💡 Quick Rule

If you can answer "When exactly?" → use Past Simple.
If the exact time doesn't matter or isn't known → use Present Perfect.
